Cancer Treatment in Turkey: Questions International Patients Ask

About this center

The Oncology Center with a partner hospital in Istanbul offers advanced diagnosis and treatment for people with cancer. A dedicated team plans each case using modern imaging, molecular diagnostics and the full range of surgical, drug and radiation options. Most international cases start by reading the documents the patient already has, before anything is booked.

Documents to send for an oncology review

  • Biopsy or pathology report, with block and slide numbers
  • Immunohistochemistry and molecular or genetic test results, if done
  • Imaging reports (PET-CT, CT, MRI), with images on disc or DICOM if possible
  • Recent blood tests and tumor markers
  • A dated list of previous treatment: surgery, chemotherapy cycles, radiotherapy and the response
  • The latest letter or discharge summary from your doctor

Send what you have: photos and scanned copies are fine to start. Your coordinator tells you if the team needs anything else.

Review before you travel

Have your file reviewed before you book anything

Send the biopsy report (with block and slide numbers), imaging on disc or DICOM, radiology reports, recent blood tests and a dated list of previous treatment. A medical oncologist reads the file with pathology and radiology, and tells you whether the diagnosis is confirmed, which options apply and whether travelling would change your plan.

In short: cancer treatment rests on four pillars: drug treatment, surgery, radiotherapy and supportive care. Which of them a case needs, and in what order, is decided by a tumor board, not by one doctor alone. We also tell you clearly when continuing care closer to home is the better choice.

How cancer is diagnosed and staged

Imaging

PET/CT, SPECT/CT, 3T MRI and modern CT.

Biopsy & pathology

Slides and blocks from home may be re-read.

Molecular & genetic testing

Genetic tests to guide targeted therapy and counseling.

Blood tests & tumor markers

Comprehensive blood work and tumor markers for follow-up.

The pillars of treatment

Medical oncology

Chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted and hormone therapy, mostly outpatient.

Radiation oncology

IMRT and VMAT on a modern linear accelerator.

Surgical oncology

Minimally invasive and robotic surgery where suitable.

Nuclear medicine & interventional oncology

Radionuclide therapies, TARE, TACE and ablation in selected cases.

Decisions made by a multidisciplinary tumor board

No single specialist decides alone. The board reviews each case together, from imaging to pathology, to combine surgical, drug and radiation treatment in the right order. One case, five specialties, one shared plan.

Cellular therapy and CAR-T

CAR-T cell therapy is available with a partner hospital in Istanbul for eligible patients, within the hematology and bone marrow transplant program and after evaluation by the team. It is used within a planned treatment pathway, results vary from patient to patient, and it is not a guaranteed cure.

  • Autologous and allogeneic bone marrow transplant for adults and children, with isolation rooms.
  • CAR-T cell therapy: available for eligible patients; the team decides eligibility after reviewing your file.
  • No promise of cure: results vary and treatment carries serious risks, explained by the physician before any decision.

Length of stay: for major oncologic surgery, plan for around 10–21 days in Türkiye; after robotic or laparoscopic surgery, the hospital stay is usually 2–5 days. Chemotherapy is usually given on an outpatient basis.

Questions patients and families ask

Can the team review my reports before I decide to travel?

Yes. Send your file; a medical oncologist reads it with pathology and radiology and replies with a written opinion on suitable options, including when staying at home is better.

Which documents should I send for an oncology review?

The biopsy or pathology report with block and slide numbers, imaging reports (PET-CT, CT or MRI) with the images on disc or DICOM if possible, recent blood tests and tumor markers, a dated list of previous treatment, and the latest letter from your doctor.

Will my biopsy slides be read again in Istanbul?

Often yes, when needed. That is why we ask for block and slide numbers; you may be asked to bring the blocks themselves.

Can I continue chemotherapy started in another country?

In many cases yes, after reviewing the previous protocol and recent tests. Your treating physician decides.

How long will I need to stay in Istanbul?

It depends on the plan: major surgery needs a longer stay than chemotherapy, which is usually given on an outpatient basis. The team gives you an estimate for your case before you travel.

Does CAR-T cell therapy mean a complete cure?

A cure cannot be promised. Some patients achieve long remission; others do not respond or relapse. Your treating physician decides whether it suits your case.

Who decides my treatment plan?

A multidisciplinary tumor board; your treating physician then discusses the plan with you and your family before starting.

How much does cancer treatment cost?

We do not publish prices. The cost depends on the type and stage of the cancer and on the treatment plan, and is assessed case by case after your medical report has been reviewed.
