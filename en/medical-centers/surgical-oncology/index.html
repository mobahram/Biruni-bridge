About this center

At Biruni Bridge, we work with the Biruni University Hospital team to help international patients prepare for cancer surgery in Istanbul. Whether to operate, when and how is decided by a multidisciplinary tumor board, because surgery may come before or after drug treatment or radiotherapy. We ask for your imaging and biopsy reports first, so the surgical team can tell you whether surgery is a suitable option before any travel is booked.

Main areas of cancer surgery

The General Surgery department at Biruni University Hospital lists tumor surgery, liver, pancreas and bile-duct surgery, gastrointestinal surgery and breast surgery among its areas. The summaries below explain what each area usually involves; the exact operation depends on your reports.

01Pancreatic surgery

Surgery for pancreatic cancer and for cystic and neuroendocrine tumors of the pancreas. Depending on where the tumor is, this may be a pancreaticoduodenectomy (Whipple operation: removal of the head of the pancreas, the duodenum and part of the bile duct) or a distal pancreatectomy (removal of the body and tail). Imaging shows whether the tumor involves nearby blood vessels, which decides whether surgery can come first.

02Liver and bile-duct surgery

Liver resection (removal of the part of the liver that contains the tumor) for primary liver tumors and selected liver metastases (cancer that has spread to the liver), and surgery for gallbladder and bile-duct cancers. Before planning, the team checks how much healthy liver will remain after the operation. Several surgeons on the team also work in the hospital's liver transplant center.

03Stomach surgery

Gastrectomy (removal of part or all of the stomach) with the nearby lymph nodes (lymphadenectomy). For many stomach cancers, the tumor board recommends chemotherapy before surgery (neoadjuvant treatment), and sometimes after it.

04Colon and rectal surgery

Removal of the affected segment of the colon or rectum with its lymph nodes, laparoscopic or robot-assisted where suitable. For rectal cancer, treatment often starts with chemoradiotherapy before surgery. Some patients need a temporary stoma (an opening of the bowel on the abdominal wall); if this is likely, the surgeon explains it in advance.

05Breast cancer surgery

Breast-conserving surgery or mastectomy, including nipple-sparing mastectomy and oncoplastic techniques (removing the tumor and reshaping the breast in the same operation), within a plan that also includes drug treatment and radiotherapy.

Every case is discussed by a multidisciplinary tumor board

At Biruni University Hospital, cancer cases are discussed at tumor boards where general surgery, medical oncology, radiation oncology, pathology and radiology specialists meet to agree on one treatment plan (protocol) and on the order of each step. This is why the plan you receive may combine surgery with drug treatment or radiotherapy.

Evaluation before surgery

  • Review of recent imaging (CT, MRI or PET-CT) and the biopsy report; older scans are repeated to confirm the stage.
  • Anesthesia assessment with heart and lung tests and blood tests.
  • Nutrition assessment, because nutrition affects recovery after major abdominal surgery.
  • Review of your medicines, especially blood thinners that may need to be paused.
  • A meeting with the surgeon to explain the planned operation, its risks and the expected recovery, with a coordinator who speaks your language.

Follow-up after surgery

The final pathology report (the laboratory examination of the removed tissue) shows whether the tumor was fully removed and whether lymph nodes were involved. The tumor board uses it to decide whether further treatment, such as chemotherapy or radiotherapy, is needed. Follow-up usually combines visits, blood tests and imaging at intervals set by the treating team; much of it can be coordinated with your doctor at home, and we stay in contact with you throughout.

Length of stay: for major surgery plan for around 10–21 days in Türkiye; after robotic or laparoscopic surgery the hospital stay is usually 2–5 days.

Questions patients ask

Can the surgical team review my reports before I travel?

Yes. Send your imaging, biopsy report and your doctor's letter. The surgical team reviews them with the tumor board and explains whether surgery is a suitable option or whether another treatment should come first.

Which documents should I send?

The biopsy or pathology report, recent CT, MRI or PET-CT reports with the images on a disc or in DICOM format if possible, recent blood tests and tumor markers, and a dated list of previous treatments.

Will I need chemotherapy or radiotherapy as well as surgery?

It depends on the tumor type and stage. Some cancers, such as many stomach and rectal cancers, are treated before surgery to shrink the tumor; others need treatment after surgery, depending on the final pathology report. The tumor board decides the order.

Can the operation be done laparoscopically or with a robot?

In suitable cases. Laparoscopic and robot-assisted surgery use small incisions, which often means less pain and a shorter hospital stay. The surgeon decides based on the tumor's size and location and your general health.

How long will I need to stay in Istanbul?

For major cancer surgery, plan for around 10–21 days in Türkiye. After laparoscopic or robotic surgery, the hospital stay itself is usually 2–5 days. The team gives an estimate for your case before you travel.

When can I fly home after surgery?

Your surgeon decides, based on the operation and how your recovery goes. Before you leave, you receive a discharge summary and instructions for your doctor at home.

How is follow-up arranged after I return home?

You receive the final pathology report and a written follow-up plan. Check-ups, blood tests and imaging can often be done with your doctor at home, with results sent to the team in Istanbul through your coordinator.
