Gene therapy and CAR-T cell therapy: how they differ

A plain explanation of gene therapy and CAR-T cell therapy for international patients comparing the two before they send a file.

1 November 2026

Prepared by Biruni Bridge in coordination with the Biruni Hospital team. Last updated 2 November 2026.

What is gene therapy?

Gene therapy is a way of treating disease by modifying a gene or the biological properties of living cells. It may replace a gene that is not working, inactivate a gene that is causing harm, or introduce a new or modified gene. It is studied in some cancers, some inherited diseases, and other diseases. It is not one single treatment, and many approaches are still in research. Whether any option fits a particular person is decided by the medical team after they read the file.

What is CAR-T cell therapy?

CAR-T cell therapy is a form of immunotherapy that uses T cells. Doctors collect T cells, usually the patient's own, modify them in a laboratory so they carry a receptor (a chimeric antigen receptor, or CAR) that can recognize a target on cancer cells, and give the cells back in an infusion. It is used mainly for certain blood cancers, often when the disease has returned or has not responded to other treatment.

What is the difference between gene therapy and CAR-T?

Gene therapy is the broader idea: changing genetic material, or the properties of living cells, in order to treat a disease. CAR-T is one application of that idea. Not every gene therapy is CAR-T. Delivering a gene into the body with a vector, for example for an inherited disease, is a different procedure from collecting T cells, modifying them, and returning them.

Is CAR-T a type of gene therapy?

CAR-T is a type of genetically modified cell therapy. Public agencies describe it as a treatment in which T cells are genetically modified in a laboratory. The FDA describes a class of gene therapy products made from a patient's own cells: the cells are removed, genetically modified, and returned. CAR-T fits that description. That does not make CAR-T the same procedure as every other gene therapy.

Who is usually evaluated for each?

For gene therapy in the broad sense, the people reviewed are those with a disease for which an approach that changes genes is authorized or under study. The review looks at the diagnosis, any genetic finding, previous care, and general health. Not every person with an inherited disease or a cancer is a candidate. For CAR-T, the people reviewed are mainly those with certain blood cancers, adults or children, when the disease has returned or has not responded. At a partner hospital in Istanbul that review is done in the Biruni Cell Therapy program. The medical team decides. Sometimes the appropriate step is to continue care at home.

What does a search for gene therapy in Turkey mean here?

Biruni Bridge does not deliver the treatment. It arranges a case review with the medical team at a partner hospital in Istanbul. The team can say whether the file fits a program they offer, including CAR-T through Biruni Cell Therapy, or whether care should continue at home. Looking for gene therapy in Turkey does not, by itself, mean that travel is the right next step.

What documents should I send for a case review?

Send the latest medical report, the pathology or biopsy report, imaging reports, recent blood tests, and a dated list of previous treatments and how the disease responded. If the question is a gene therapy other than CAR-T, add the genetic report when you have one. A coordinator who speaks your language confirms how to send the file. You can also leave your details through the contact page on this website.

How does Biruni Bridge help?

Biruni Bridge is an independent facilitator. The coordinator receives the file, the medical team at the partner hospital reads it, and you receive a written view, including when the appropriate step is not to travel. Coordination can include interpretation, a medical invitation letter for a visa, airport transfer, a place to stay for the patient and one companion, and follow up with the doctor at home. You can write on WhatsApp at +90 536 842 55 97 or by email at info@birunibridge.com. No result is promised.

Does this page replace your doctor's advice?

No. This is general information. The physician treating you decides what is suitable in your case. For CAR-T in the international patient pathway, see the CAR-T page or the contact form.

Sources

  • FDA. What is Gene Therapy? Describes gene therapy, including products made from a patient's own cells that are genetically modified and then returned.
  • FDA. Cellular & Gene Therapy Products The FDA page that groups cellular and gene therapy products.
  • National Cancer Institute. CAR T Cells: Engineering Patients' Immune Cells to Treat Their Cancers Describes collecting a patient's T cells, engineering them to carry a chimeric antigen receptor, and giving them back.
  • National Cancer Institute. T-cell Transfer Therapy Describes CAR-T as a type of T cell transfer therapy, in which T cells are changed in the laboratory so they can bind to cancer cells.
