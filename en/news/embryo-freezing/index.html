Embryo freezing in Turkey: questions couples ask before IVF

Embryo freezing lets a couple keep good quality embryos from one IVF cycle and use them in a later transfer.

2 November 2026

Reviewed by the Biruni Hospital medical team, 2 November 2026.

This page answers the usual questions in order: what freezing is, when it is suggested, how the steps run, what Turkish law allows, and how long embryos can be stored. We at Biruni Bridge work in coordination with the Biruni Hospital team in Istanbul. Biruni Bridge is an independent facilitator, not the hospital, and does not provide treatment itself.

What is embryo freezing?

An IVF cycle often produces more good quality embryos than the first transfer needs. These embryos can be frozen and kept for later [3]. The method used today is mostly vitrification: very fast freezing that stops ice crystals from forming and damaging the embryo. The embryos are then stored in liquid nitrogen [3] at about −196 °C [7].

When is freezing suggested?

  • Good quality embryos are left over after the transfer [2].
  • The first transfer does not lead to a pregnancy, or the couple wants another child later. A frozen embryo can then be used without repeating the injections and the egg retrieval [3].
  • The ovaries respond very strongly to stimulation. The doctor may then freeze all embryos instead of doing a fresh transfer, to lower the risk of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) [2].
  • Freezing allows the transfer to take place when the lining of the uterus is well prepared [1][3].
  • Before chemotherapy or radiotherapy, eggs or embryos can be frozen so that the chance of having children is not lost [7].

In Turkey, embryo freezing is open only to legally married couples [4].

What are the steps?

  1. Assessment. Both partners are assessed. For the wife: hormone tests, ultrasound and sometimes an imaging test of the uterus with contrast dye. For the husband: a semen analysis [1][2].
  2. Ovarian stimulation. Daily hormone injections, with ultrasound and blood tests to follow the eggs [1][2]. This stage takes about 10–14 days [1].
  3. Egg retrieval. The eggs are collected with a thin needle guided by ultrasound, usually under light anaesthesia [1]. It takes about 20–30 minutes, and the wife goes back to her hotel the same day [2].
  4. Fertilisation. In the laboratory, the eggs are fertilised with the husband's sperm, by standard IVF or by ICSI [1][2].
  5. Embryo monitoring. Embryo development is followed for 2 to 5 days [1].
  6. Freezing. Good quality embryos are vitrified and stored in liquid nitrogen [2][3].
  7. Preparing the uterus and transfer. In a later month, the uterus is prepared again, often with oestrogen for the lining [1]. The embryo is thawed and placed with a thin catheter. This is painless and takes a few minutes [1].
  8. Pregnancy test. A blood test about 10–14 days after the transfer [1][2].

Is a frozen transfer as successful as a fresh one?

According to Biruni University Hospital, the success rates of frozen embryo transfer may be close to those of fresh transfer [1]. Current methods give good survival rates for embryos after thawing, but success is never certain [3]. Results depend on several factors, mainly the woman's age, her health and her response to treatment [1]. More precisely, what counts is her age when the eggs were retrieved, not her age at transfer [7].

We do not publish success rates. The doctor explains your chances after reading your reports.

What does Turkish law allow?

  • IVF and embryo freezing are allowed only for legally married couples, using the wife's eggs and the husband's sperm [2][4].
  • Egg, sperm and embryo donation are prohibited, and so is surrogacy [2][5].
  • Embryos may not be given to anyone other than the couple, and the law sets penalties for doing so [5].
  • Freezing surplus embryos requires the consent of both spouses [4].
  • Frozen embryos can be moved to another centre inside Turkey on a written application by both spouses [4].
  • An apostilled marriage certificate with a certified translation is required [2].

How long can embryos be stored?

According to Biruni Hospital, embryos are stored for five years, and storage can be extended by another five years, so ten years in total. The extension needs the couple's request and the approval of the competent authorities [2][4][6].

The regulation used to require a signed request from the couple every year once storage passed one year. The Council of State (Danıştay) annulled this requirement on 17 April 2025 [6]. Some centres may still ask couples to renew their consent each year, so ask the centre exactly what it needs and keep your contact details up to date with it.

What affects the cost?

The cost differs between centres and between cases. It depends on ICSI, genetic testing of the embryos where there is a medical reason, the medication dose, the number of monitoring visits, the freezing itself and a yearly storage fee [2]. Ask for a written, itemised quote before you start. We do not publish prices.
